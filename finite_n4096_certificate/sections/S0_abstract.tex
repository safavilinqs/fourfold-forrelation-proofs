We consider a four-block binary-phase sensing task with $N=4096$ modes per block, where every photon traversal of an unknown phase coordinate is charged, including traversals preceding discarded or no-click outcomes. The task distinguishes four-fold forrelation at least $1/4$ from four-fold forrelation at most $-1/4$. We prove that every classically adaptive passive protocol of hard dose at most six has worst-case promised error greater than $1/3$ when each fresh batch is block diagonal in total signal photon number. The class permits arbitrary idlers, repeated modes, within-sector entanglement and coherence, collective measurements, and unrestricted classical feed-forward, but no coherent quantum memory between batches. The passive proof combines an attenuated signed-permutation hard pair, 888 physical occurrence bounds, an outward 210-state spectral certificate, and a normalized adaptive factorization, giving transcript distance at most $0.2609692247922079$. Coherence between different signal-number sectors, including vacuum--nonvacuum coherence, is not covered. An active protocol retains one photon coherently across two charged phase grids; three independent flags and majority decoding use hard dose six and have worst-case error $81/256$. The ideal protocol requires $16{,}384$ sign coordinates and retained contrast above $0.904294855157$, and current platform evidence does not certify an implementation at this scale.
