\section{The one-batch certificate}
\label{sec:one-batch}

\subsection{Occurrence expansion}

A dose-six batch is expanded in Fock occupations on the four sign banks. When a ket occupation and a bra occupation are compared under the hard pair, their symmetric difference produces a profile $a=(a_1,a_2,a_3,a_4)$ of total degree between four and twelve, and a split $t$ records which occurrences lie on one side of the matrix. Exact occupation compatibility is

\begin{equation}
m=n+a-2t,
\label{eq:onebatch-compatibility}
\end{equation}

where $n$ and $m$ are the four-block ket and bra occupations. The state space contains every occupation with total dose at most six. Because Eq.~\eqref{eq:model-number-sector} removes ket--bra terms of different total signal photon number, a contributing profile--split pair must satisfy $2|t|=|a|$. Equation~\eqref{eq:onebatch-compatibility} then preserves total occupation.

The upstream routing inventory contains 6,016 high-sector profile--split records. Exactly 888 satisfy the number-sector balance condition, and every one of those records has a proved coefficient. The remaining 5,128 routing values are excluded from the theorem ledger rather than called coefficient bounds. This distinction matters: 272 of those unbalanced profile splits would create split/state incidences, comprising 136 undirected edges between different-total-number states, if coherence between number sectors were allowed. Their physical coefficients are not certified by this package.

As a concrete occupation example, take

\begin{equation}
n=(1,1,1,1),\quad
a=(1,1,1,1),\quad
t=(1,0,1,0).
\end{equation}

Equation~\eqref{eq:onebatch-compatibility} gives $m=(0,2,0,2)$, so both sides have dose four because $2|t|=|a|=4$. The four coordinates in these vectors are photon totals in the four sign banks rather than positions within a bank; the physical positions and their distinctness masks remain inside the occurrence matrix $\mathcal O_{a,t}$.

\subsection{Physical coefficients}

For a fixed profile and split, the physical occurrence matrix retains the within-block simple-support constraints and every cross-cut distinctness mask. Its accepted coefficient $c_{a,t}$ bounds

\begin{equation}
\sup_{p,w}
\left\|
\diag(\sqrt p)\,\mathcal O_{a,t}\,\diag(\sqrt w)
\right\|_1
\le c_{a,t},
\label{eq:onebatch-coefficient}
\end{equation}

for arbitrary correlated diagonal row and column laws $p,w$. Product laws, uniformity, exchangeability, translation invariance, and separate optimization of different links are not assumed.

The physical coefficient proofs are grouped into masked quintic slices, local Walsh contractions, cubic and quintic endpoint factorizations, finite incidence theorems, shared-quintic contractions, a final residual-chain theorem, and an independently audited dual-endpoint theorem. Earlier coefficient-one arguments that omitted cross-cut masks are absent from the accepted registry. Appendix~\ref{app:ledger} gives the status counts and source map.

\subsection{Outward coefficient rounding}

Every accepted nonnegative binary64 coefficient $c$ is replaced by

\begin{equation}
\widehat c
=\frac{\lceil10^9c\rceil+1}{10^9}>c.
\label{eq:onebatch-grid}
\end{equation}

The extra grid unit makes the replacement strict and limits the inflation to less than $2\times10^{-9}$. Coefficients supplied as exact rational squares are checked separately against the squares of their outward ledger values.

\subsection{Occupation matrix}

There are 210 four-block nonnegative occupation vectors of total dose at most six. The rounded coefficients and attenuation weights assemble into a symmetric entrywise nonnegative matrix $B$ on these states. The occurrence contribution for normalized occupation laws is bounded by the spectral norm of $B$, which equals its Perron root.

A floating eigensolve of the balanced matrix supplies a strictly positive candidate vector $v$ and does not supply the accepted eigenvalue. The certified value uses the Collatz--Wielandt inequality

\begin{equation}
\rho(B)
\le
\max_i\frac{(\widehat Bv)_i}{v_i},
\label{eq:onebatch-collatz}
\end{equation}

where $\widehat B$ is reconstructed entrywise with 80-digit directed arithmetic and outward square roots. This gives

\begin{equation}
P_{\mathrm{Perron}}
\le 0.258744096385577223.
\label{eq:onebatch-perron}
\end{equation}

Adding Eq.~\eqref{eq:hard-promise} gives the one-batch outward total in Eq.~\eqref{eq:statement-total}. The remaining question is whether arbitrary classical outcome trees whose fresh probes obey Eq.~\eqref{eq:model-number-sector} preserve the coefficient and spectral normalizations; Sec.~\ref{sec:adaptive} supplies that lift.
