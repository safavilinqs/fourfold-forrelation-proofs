# Scope review

Status: PASS after correction of the total-signal-number scope.

The abstract, model, theorem, adaptive section, and discussion state that the passive lower bound covers fresh batches block diagonal in total signal photon number, with arbitrary mixtures of number sectors, within-sector entanglement and coherence, idlers, repeated modes, collective measurements, rare outcomes, branch-dependent dose partitions, and unrestricted classical feed-forward. It excludes cross-number-sector coherence as well as coherent quantum memory and control carried between batches. The cost is branchwise hard photon-pass dose.

The earlier claim covering vacuum--nonvacuum and other cross-number-sector coherence was rejected. The at-most-six occupation graph contains 272 unbalanced high-sector split/state incidences on 136 different-number edges, and their routing placeholders were not theorem coefficients. The corrected ledger excludes them and the manuscript marks the broader extension open.

The signed-permutation distributions are presented as a hard-pair witness rather than as a characterization of every input distribution. The active result is pointwise on the promise sets and is not inferred from the passive hard distribution.

The manuscript states that active dose five, the optimal passive dose, the $N=1024$ finite-size target, and passive protocols with coherent inter-batch quantum memory remain unresolved. The implementation section grades the contrast threshold as a derived specification and the platform comparison as experimental evidence; it does not claim that the $16{,}384$ sign coordinates, receiver, loss budget, or conditioning rates have been realized.

The active schematic and signed-permutation example use two-dimensional phase grids to represent the logical factorization $4096=64^2$. Their captions state that this indexing does not require a square spatial device, and neither figure is presented as evidence of experimental feasibility.

No scope blocker remains for the corrected restricted theorem. The broader passive extension is an explicit open problem.
