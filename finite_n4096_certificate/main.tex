\begin{abstract}
Three single photons, each crossing two phase masks, decide the
fourfold-forrelation promise with hard dose six and error $81/256$.
The recorded finite certificate excludes a single parallel probe of
dose six whose state is block diagonal in total signal photon number.
Its comparison is between a two-pass interferometer and one parallel
interrogation. A previously claimed extension to classically adaptive
fresh probes has a norm-interface gap and is not established here.
We give the short analytic argument, the exact interface to the
numerical certificate, and the remaining proof obligation.
\end{abstract}

\section{The comparison}
For a power of two $N$, let
$H_{ij}=N^{-1/2}(-1)^{i\cdot j}$ and
$u=N^{-1/2}(1,\ldots,1)^{\mathsf T}$. Four unknown binary phase masks
$D_b=\diag(x^{(b)})$, $x^{(b)}\in\{\pm1\}^N$, define
\begin{equation}
 F(x)=\Forr(x)=u^{\mathsf T}D_1HD_2HD_3HD_4u .
 \label{eq:task}
\end{equation}
Decide $F\ge1/4$ versus $F\le-1/4$, with worst-case error at most $1/3$.
This is a variant of the query problem of
Aaronson and Ambainis~\cite{AaronsonAmbainis2015};
a classical-query lower bound is not a bound on the quantum probes below.

A parallel probe is fixed before observing any sample output, undergoes
one direct-sum mask interaction, and is measured by an arbitrary POVM.
It may have arbitrary bypass idlers, repeated signal modes, entanglement,
and coherence within a fixed total signal-number sector. We require
\begin{equation}
 [\rho,\widehat N_{\rm sig}]=0,\qquad
 \rho\text{ supported on }\widehat N_{\rm sig}\le6 .
 \label{eq:number}
\end{equation}
Thus mixtures of different total numbers are allowed, but coherence
between them is not. Dose counts every photon-mask traversal; it is a
support bound, not a mean or a postselected cost.
Several predetermined batches of total dose six also fit this model
by joint preparation and a final collective measurement.

\begin{theorem}[Finite parallel comparison]\label{thm:finite}
At $N=4096$, the recorded occurrence certificate gives two laws
$P_+,P_-$ supported on the respective promises such that every
parallel probe satisfying \eqref{eq:number} has output laws obeying
\begin{equation}
 \TV(T_+,T_-)\le0.260969224792207925 .
 \label{eq:total}
\end{equation}
Its equal-prior Bayes error is therefore at least
$0.369515387603896037>1/3$.
A multipass protocol has hard dose six and error $81/256<1/3$.
\end{theorem}

The analytic proof reduces the lower bound to explicit occurrence
coefficients stored with their supporting derivations. Section~\ref{sec:ledger}
specifies this interface and its arithmetic verification.
The package was developed with substantial language-model assistance.
The coefficient families and their reduction to physical occupations
remain subject to independent mathematical review; successful replay
checks the recorded calculation rather than every analytic lemma.

\section{The two-pass protocol}
Define
\[
 L_x=D_2HD_1u,\qquad R_x=HD_3HD_4u.
\]
Prepare a single photon in
$(|0\rangle L_x+|1\rangle R_x)/\sqrt2$.
Each arm crosses exactly two masks. Recombining the paths gives
\begin{equation}
 \Pr(X=\pm1\mid x)=\frac{1\pm\langle L_x,R_x\rangle}{2}
                 =\frac{1\pm F(x)}2.
 \label{eq:flag}
\end{equation}
The mode label can be discarded; only the output port is needed.
At either promise boundary, a flag is correct with probability $5/8$.
Three independent flags and majority decoding have error
\[
 (3/8)^3+3(5/8)(3/8)^2=81/256,
\]
and deterministic dose $3\cdot2=6$. No outcome is discarded.
This proves the upper bound for every promised input and every $N$.

\section{The hard inputs}
Set $q=64$, so $N=q^2$. Identify a coordinate with $(a,b)\in[q]^2$,
and use row-major flattening, so $H_N=H_q\otimes H_q$.
Write $S_q=\sqrt q\,H_q$.
For a uniform permutation $\pi$ and independent uniform signs $\sigma_b$,
define arrays
\begin{equation}
 L_{a,b}=S_q(a,\pi(b))\sigma_b,\qquad
 R_{\pi(b),a}=\sigma_bS_q(b,a).
 \label{eq:lr}
\end{equation}
Their useful property is simply
\begin{equation}
                         H_N L=R,\qquad H_N R=L.
 \label{eq:hlr}
\end{equation}
Indeed, at coordinate $(c,d)$,
\[
 (H_NL)_{c,d}
 =\frac1q\sum_{a,b}S_q(c,a)S_q(d,b)S_q(a,\pi(b))\sigma_b
 =S_q(d,b_c)\sigma_{b_c}=R_{c,d},
\]
where $b_c=\pi^{-1}(c)$. The second identity follows from $H_N^2=I$.

Take three independent pairs $(L_j,R_j)$ and set
\begin{equation}
 z=(L_1,\ R_1\odot L_2,\ R_2\odot L_3,\ R_3).
 \label{eq:plant}
\end{equation}
Starting on the right of \eqref{eq:task}, \eqref{eq:hlr} successively
cancels $R_3,L_3,R_2,L_2,R_1,L_1$ and leaves $u$.
Thus $F(z)=1$ exactly. Negating the first block changes the value to $-1$.

Now independently flip every coordinate with probability $3/25$.
Equivalently, multiply it by a sign $\eta$ with mean
\[
                         \beta=19/25.
\]
Let $\widetilde P_+$ be the resulting positive law and
$\widetilde P_-$ its first-block reflection.
Then $\E_{\widetilde P_+}F=\beta^4$.
Finally condition each law onto its own promise to obtain $P_+,P_-$.

\begin{lemma}[Cost of conditioning]\label{lem:promise}
The conditioning loss is at most
$0.002225128406630703$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix an exact plant $z$. Reveal the four noisy blocks in order from
left to right and form the Doob martingale of $F$.
When block $b$ is exposed, the future $4-b$ masks contribute their
means, giving a factor $\beta^{4-b}$. By \eqref{eq:hlr}, the remaining
deterministic right-hand chain is a sign vector divided by $\sqrt N$.
The left-hand chain has norm one.
The sum of squared coefficients of the newly exposed signs is therefore
$\beta^{2(4-b)}/N$.

A centered sign in $[-1,1]$ has subgaussian proxy at most one by
Hoeffding's lemma. Conditional independence within each newly exposed
block gives the martingale proxy
\[
 V_\beta=\frac{1+\beta^2+\beta^4+\beta^6}{N}
        =\frac{(1+\beta^2)(1+\beta^4)}N .
\]
With $g=\beta^4-1/4$ and $x=g^2/(2V_\beta)$, Chernoff gives a
failure probability at most $e^{-x}$. This holds for every plant.
Reflection gives the same bound on the negative side.
The verifier evaluates the exact rational upper
\[
 2e^{-x}\le2(1+x/4096)^{-4096}
          <0.002225128406630703 .
\]
\end{proof}

\section{From a quantum probe to one matrix}\label{sec:matrix}
This is where the single-batch hypothesis is used.
Purify the input and write
$\psi=\sum_\nu|\nu\rangle\alpha_\nu$, where $\nu$ is a physical
Fock occupation, including repeated modes.
Put $p_\nu=\|\alpha_\nu\|^2$ and let
$\chi_\nu(x)=\prod_{b,i}(x_i^{(b)})^{\nu_{b,i}}$.
For the unconditioned hard laws, define the half-difference moment matrix
\begin{equation}
 K_{\nu\mu}=\frac12
   (\E_{\widetilde P_+}-\E_{\widetilde P_-})
                          [\chi_\nu\chi_\mu].
 \label{eq:moment}
\end{equation}
The map $|\nu\rangle\mapsto|\nu\rangle\alpha_\nu/\sqrt{p_\nu}$ is
an isometry on nonzero weights. Therefore the trace norm of half the
difference of the averaged quantum output states is exactly
\begin{equation}
                  \|\diag(\sqrt p)K\diag(\sqrt p)\|_1 .
 \label{eq:trace}
\end{equation}
Any subsequent measurement has total variation no larger than
\eqref{eq:trace}. Mixed inputs are handled by convexity and sector
refinement. This proves the reduction for arbitrary idlers and POVMs.

The independent permutation signs make \eqref{eq:moment} vanish unless
the symmetric-difference degree is odd in every block. A surviving
profile $a=(a_1,\ldots,a_4)$ therefore has degree four through twelve.
Let $t_b$ count its ket occurrences in block $b$.
If $n,m$ are the four block totals of the physical occupations, then
\begin{equation}
                 m=n+a-2t,\qquad 2|t|=|a|.
 \label{eq:compat}
\end{equation}
The second equality follows from equal total signal number in
\eqref{eq:number}. Physical mode positions and distinctness constraints
remain in the occurrence kernel $\mathcal O_{a,t}$; $n,m,a,t$ are only
four-component counts.

\section{The finite coefficient certificate}\label{sec:ledger}
For each contributing profile and split, the repository supplies a
coefficient $c_{a,t}$ for the complete physical occurrence kernel:
\begin{equation}
 \sup_{p,w}
 \|\diag(\sqrt p)\mathcal O_{a,t}\diag(\sqrt w)\|_1\le c_{a,t}.
 \label{eq:coefficient}
\end{equation}
Here $p,w$ are arbitrary normalized row and column laws.
The kernels include repeated-mode reduction, simple supports, and
every distinctness mask crossing the row--column split.
Discarding those masks was an error in an earlier coefficient proof.

The supporting derivations are retained in
\path{code/source_snapshot/}. The entry point
\path{code/COEFFICIENTS.md} maps the accepted families to their proof
notes and executable formulas. The 888 balanced high-sector entries
supplement the inherited lower-degree bounds.
Of 6,016 high-sector routing entries, 5,128 are unbalanced and are
excluded rather than used as coefficients. There are 272 unbalanced
split/state incidences, forming 136 undirected edges in the larger
cross-number space; they are a genuine missing case for number coherence.

Here is the complete interface between \eqref{eq:coefficient} and
the numerical answer. Let
\[
 \mathcal N=\{n\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^4:|n|\le6\},
 \qquad |\mathcal N|={10\choose4}=210,\qquad
 b(n,t)=\prod_{j=1}^4{n_j\choose t_j}.
\]
For every balanced $(a,t)$ and compatible $(n,m)$ in
\eqref{eq:compat}, add
\begin{equation}
 \beta^{|a|}\widehat c_{a,t}
                 \sqrt{b(n,t)b(m,a-t)}
 \label{eq:edge}
\end{equation}
to $A_{nm}$, and put $B=(A+A^{\mathsf T})/2$.
The factor $\beta^{|a|}$ is the attenuation of the surviving signs.
The binomial factors count ways to select their photon occurrences.

The occurrence expansion, the trace-norm triangle inequality and
\eqref{eq:coefficient} bound \eqref{eq:trace} by $v^{\mathsf T}Bv$,
where $v_n$ is the square root of the probe's probability of block
occupation $n$. Since $\|v\|_2=1$, this is at most $\rho(B)$, the
Perron root. The matrix is nonnegative, symmetric and block diagonal
in total photon number. This is a single-batch statement; no adaptive
normalization has been inserted.

For nonzero binary64 source coefficients $c$, the numerical code uses
$\widehat c=(\lceil10^9c\rceil+1)/10^9$; zero coefficients stay zero.
This rounds the supplied floating values outward. Analytic bounds
must still be justified by the family derivations; rounding alone
does not turn a floating formula into an analytic certificate.
The dual-endpoint family additionally checks exact squared bounds.

A floating eigensolve provides only a strictly positive candidate $w$.
The accepted bound is the outward Collatz ratio
\[
 \rho(B)\le\max_i\frac{(\widehat Bw)_i}{w_i}
           <0.258744096385577223.
\]
All entries and ratios are reconstructed with directed decimal arithmetic
and outward square roots. The committed candidate makes replay
independent of eigensolver rounding.
Combining this with Lemma~\ref{lem:promise} gives \eqref{eq:total}.
The stored high-precision sum is rounded once; adding the two displayed
component bounds and rounding again would give a slightly looser value.
The Bayes identity $(1-\TV)/2$ completes Theorem~\ref{thm:finite}.

\section{The adaptive gap}
For a fresh adaptive tree, the earlier proof constructs strategy
coefficients
\[
 D(I,J)=\langle V_J,U_I\rangle,\qquad
 \sum_I\|U_I\|^2\le1,\quad \sum_J\|V_J\|^2\le1.
\]
This induction is valid. It does not justify applying
\eqref{eq:coefficient} to the \emph{sum of all entries}
\[
 \sum_{I,J}K_{f(I),f(J)}D(I,J).
\]

\begin{samepage}
The obstruction is elementary. For $n$ histories take
$K=J_n$ (the all-ones matrix) and scalar features
$U_i=V_i=1/\sqrt n$. Both squared feature masses are one and
the kernel $K$ has unit scalar Schur factors. Yet
\[
 \sum_{i,j}K_{ij}\langle V_j,U_i\rangle=n,\qquad
 \|\diag(\sqrt p)K\diag(\sqrt p)\|_1=1,\quad p_i=1/n .
\]
\end{samepage}
A Schur factorization controls the trace norm of a matrix, not its
unrestricted entry sum. The latter pairs the matrix with $J_n$,
whose operator norm is $n$.
This counterexample refutes the inference from the stated invariant.
It is not a physical adaptive strategy that refutes the forrelation
lower bound.

A valid adaptive extension needs an additional physical contraction
retaining the causal measurement structure, or a different adaptive
bound with controlled overhead. The saved multiplier-one artifact
only inserts the number one after checking the ledger; it does not
prove this missing inequality. Its old verdict is historical and is
excluded from the supported verification.

The parallel result also does not cover cross-number coherence,
a mean-dose budget, or quantum memory between encounters. It does not
prove that six is the optimal multipass dose.
