# Round 4 tests

Tests protect theorem statements, protocol values, certified intervals, and
decision-changing diagnostics.  They do not turn a numerical observation
into a proof.

The initialization test checks that:

- the exact practical goal is present;
- inherited proved facts and nonclaims are separated;
- the finite-size passive certificate and active six-dose benchmark are
  represented;
- the sharp level-twelve obstruction is preserved;
- experimental resources and feasibility are explicit completion gates;
- long-horizon active-five and exponent work is deferred; and
- the joint 16/51-orbit retain-or-pivot decision is the first project.

The finite-size ledger regression also protects the 888/40/848 evidence
split, both reoptimized coefficient gates, every inherited checkpoint total,
the 16/51-orbit impact frontier, all provenance paths, the \(N=q^2\)
implementation restriction, and byte equality between regenerated and
committed CSV/JSON artifacts. The correlated-mixture test checks the
fourteen-class reduction against the unreduced \(q=4\) formula for nonuniform
invariant laws before accepting the \(q=32\) diagnostic.

The two-axis regression separately checks a genuinely non-invariant law
against the unreduced order-four formula. It protects the optimized
order-32 horizontal mass, coefficient, remaining kill-gate headroom, and
the stop decision for local leading-orbit witness tuning.

The exact-link regression checks the sparse-support signed-permutation
moment formula against exhaustive enumeration at orders two and four and
against a closed-form order-32 case. This is the arithmetic foundation for
the compatible joint-law diagnostic; it is not itself a finite-size passive
lower-bound theorem.

The sparse joint-law regression protects the first exact compatible-law
calculation on all 16 leading unresolved families. It records why the old
order-two direct model cannot see this frontier, the size of the full
coordinate-uniform order-four basis, the deterministic sampled law, its
order-32 embedding, and the observed difference between separate and joint
trace norms.

The quadratic-bent replacement regression protects its 2,284-entry
compatible inventory, zero-higher-sector floor, common and degree-specific
coefficient gates, the \(1/(2N)\) passing calibration, the \(1/N\) failing
calibration, and the explicit decision not to promote an unproved
replacement.

The adaptive tree-frontier regression reconstructs the inherited two-copy witness exactly inside a normalized strategy factorization, checks eighty complex POVM/child direct-sum inductions, exercises a root outcome below $7\times10^{-15}$, checks repeated-mode character pullbacks for $(1,5)$, $(2,4)$, and $(3,3)$, and stress-tests one hundred duplicated-history pullbacks and independent-law Perron inequalities. It protects the multiplier-one theorem interface but does not replace the analytic proof.
