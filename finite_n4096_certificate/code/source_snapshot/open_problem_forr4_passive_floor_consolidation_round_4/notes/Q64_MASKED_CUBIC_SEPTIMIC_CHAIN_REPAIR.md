## Record-compatible physical rows

Up to path reversal, the profile is $(1,3,7,1)$. Both singleton endpoint
links force record one, while the cubic--septimic link has common record
$r\in\{1,3\}$. The proof keeps the two central record sectors separate.

For a physical degree-seven support containing a fixed $k$-cell occurrence
partial, let $D_k^{r,1}$ be the maximum number of completions with row record
$r$ and column record one. Direct finite bipartite-graph-shape counting gives

$$
\begin{array}{c|rr}

\hline
(r,1)=(1,1) & 110311919 & 16209900\\
(r,1)=(3,1) & 5152854224 & 505158144.
\end{array}
$$

These are physical completion counts. The selected and completion cells are
disjoint before the support is assigned a record, so the septimic occurrence
mask is never removed.

## The missing endpoint refinement

The old bound charged every variable-singleton septimic endpoint completion
with squared residual one. That is much too expensive in central record one.

For a degree-seven support with endpoint-axis record one, call an even
endpoint group active when the XOR of its neighboring labels is nonzero. If
there are $t$ active groups, the singleton--septimic endpoint moment has the
form

$$
M_{7,1}=\frac1q\,\omega\,J_x(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_t).
$$

The injective-Walsh estimates already proved for the signed-permutation law
give

$$
|J_x|\le 1\quad(t=0),
$$

$$
|J_x|\le\frac1{q-1}\quad(t=1,2),
$$

and

$$
|J_x|\le\frac3{(q-1)(q-3)}\quad(t=3).
$$

At $q=64$, every nonzero-active completion therefore gains a squared factor
at most $1/(q-1)^2$ after the variable singleton is summed.

It remains to count the zero-active completions. In endpoint record one and
central record one, their endpoint degree pattern must be $3+4$. A degree
seven group would force central record seven; every degree-two group is
active; and a zero-XOR degree-six group paired with a singleton leaves
central record five or seven. In the surviving $3+4$ case, write $A$ for the
three-row neighborhood and $B$ for the four-row neighborhood. Zero activity
and central record one force

$$
A\subset B,
\qquad
\bigoplus_{b\in B}b=0.
$$

Any fixed three- or four-cell occurrence partial lies in at most
$5(q-1)$ such supports. If the partial uses one endpoint coordinate, its
degree-three and degree-four roles contribute at most $(q-1)$ and
$4(q-1)$. If it uses both endpoint coordinates, the zero-XOR four-set count
containing the fixed row labels is smaller. Thus the complete variable
endpoint energy obeys

$$
B_k^{1,1}
\le
5(q-1)+\frac{D_k^{1,1}}{(q-1)^2},
\qquad k\in\{3,4\}.
$$

For central record three, the proof retains the safe bound

$$
B_k^{3,1}\le D_k^{3,1};
$$

the middle moment already contributes $1/\binom q3$.

## Complete-row coefficients

Let $F_r$ be the squared cubic endpoint energy in central record $r$. A
complete physical row with the far singleton on the completion side has
sector coefficient

$$
\sqrt{
F_r\,
\frac{B_k^{r,1}}{\binom q r^2}
}.
$$

When the far singleton is fixed, the inherited physical-incidence estimate
$D_k^{r,1}/q^2$ is used instead. The fixed-one cubic slice is also retained:

$$
F_{1,1}=\frac3{q^2},
\qquad
F_{1,3}=\frac{q^2-4}{2q^2}.
$$

Summing the two record sectors after bounding each complete row closes the
three complement/reversal orbits generated by

$$
(1,3,7,1):(0,2,4,0),
$$

$$
(1,3,7,1):(0,2,3,1),
$$

and

$$
(1,3,7,1):(0,1,4,1).
$$

Their outward-rounded coefficients are respectively

$$
0.0884219637995,
\qquad
0.409310626743,
\qquad
0.538626546351.
$$

All are below one. The cubic endpoint slice contains the cubic occurrence
mask, and both $D_k^{r,1}$ and $B_k^{r,1}$ count only physical septimic
completions. Restricting the endpoint feature rows to a record sector cannot
increase their norm. The composition is therefore a Schur-feature
factorization for arbitrary correlated row and column diagonal laws; it is
not an invariant-law or product-law calculation.
