## Complete masked row

Fix a row consisting of a pair $E$ in the first quintic, a triple $F$ in the second quintic, and the right singleton $d$. A surviving column consists of the left singleton $a$, a triple $G$ disjoint from $E$, and a pair $H$ disjoint from $F$. Thus the actual occurrence row is

$$
K_{(E,F,d),(a,G,H)}
=
M_{1,5}(a,E\cup G)
M_{5,5}(E\cup G,F\cup H)
M_{5,1}(F\cup H,d).
$$

No support is completed across a missing mask: $G\cap E=\varnothing$ and $H\cap F=\varnothing$ are imposed inside the two endpoint sums.

Let $r\in\{1,3,5\}$ be the odd parity record carried by the central link. The signed-permutation permanent formula gives

$$
|M_{5,5}(U,V)|
\le
\frac{r!(q-r)!}{q!}
=
\frac{1}{\binom qr}
$$

whenever the two central records agree, and zero when they do not.

Define $L_r$ as the maximum left endpoint squared sum over the variable singleton $a$ and all masked triple completions $G$, with a fixed pair $E$ and central record $r$. Define $R_r$ as the maximum right endpoint squared sum over all masked pair completions $H$, with fixed triple $F$, fixed singleton $d$, and central record $r$. Then every complete physical row satisfies

$$
\sum_{a,G,H}|K_{(E,F,d),(a,G,H)}|^2
\le
\sum_{r\in\{1,3,5\}}
\frac{L_rR_r}{\binom qr^2}.
$$

The standard complete-row factorization therefore bounds the arbitrary-correlated-diagonal coefficient by the square root of this quantity. For the other generators, the proof uses the same record sum with a fixed or variable singleton and one, two, or three fixed quintic cells. Complementation transposes the occurrence matrix, so each entry receives the better available complete-row or complete-column bound.

## Exact endpoint enumeration

The endpoint singleton forces the adjacent quintic record to have size one. Group a quintic support by its row multiplicities. Among its even row groups, let the nonzero column xors be empty, one value, or two values. The singleton--quintic structured permanent gives the following squared weights after summing a variable singleton:

$$
1,
\qquad
\frac{1}{(q-1)^2},
\qquad
\frac{4}{(q-1)^2(q-2)^2},
$$

where the middle case includes two equal nonzero xors. Fixing the singleton divides these weights by $q^2$.

For a fixed pair or triple, every affine shape over the binary coordinate space is determined by its row and column equality pattern. There are only three pair types. For triples, any two distinct nonzero differences are linearly independent over $\mathbb F_2$, so the equality patterns are again the complete affine list. The theorem module enumerates all of these shapes, all completion equality types, and all central records with exact rational arithmetic.

Fixing the singleton in the pair family divides $P_r$ by $q^2$, while freeing it in the triple family multiplies $T_r$ by $q^2$. The $A_r$ formula is an exact count of the column patterns $5$, $4+1$, $3+2$, and $2+2+1$, including the equal-xor subfamily.

The final orbit needs a variable singleton with four fixed quintic cells. A record-one completion is bounded by $2q$: if the four-cell partial has two odd rows, only those two rows can receive the final cell; if it has no odd rows, the row pattern is $4$ or $2+2$. Pattern $4$ cannot also reach column record one because its four columns are distinct. Pattern $2+2$ leaves a nonzero size-two row xor after every completion, so its total singleton weight is at most $q^2/(q-1)^2<2$. Four odd rows cannot reach record one. For central records three and five, the trivial completion and weight bound is $q^2$. Thus

$$
(B_1,B_3,B_5)
\le
(2q,q^2,q^2).
$$
