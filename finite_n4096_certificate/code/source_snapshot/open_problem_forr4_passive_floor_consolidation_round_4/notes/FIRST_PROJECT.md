# First project: joint retain-or-pivot test

Date: 2026-07-15

Status: historical and superseded. The witness was retained provisionally,
the live size moved to $N=4096$, and the current bounded project is the
164-entry shared-quintic/adaptive gate in
Q64_SHARED_QUINTIC_AND_ADAPTIVE_ACCEPTANCE.md.

## Question

Can the attenuated signed-permutation hard pair survive one compatible
physical-law test and one shared contraction across the highest-impact
unresolved families at \(N=1024\)?

If yes, use the shared result to close the one-batch ledger before opening
adaptivity. If no, stop the scalar route and instantiate a replacement hard
instance. This project decides the witness; the broader Round 4 goal still
requires the finite-size adaptive theorem and active resource statement.

## Starting evidence

Round 3 leaves:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{conditioned degree-eight total}&=0.2482247496,\\
\text{proved partial total}&=0.2797585469,\\
\text{coarse completion diagnostic}&=0.3331326055,\\
\text{diagnostic margin}&=0.0002007278.
\end{aligned}
\tag{0.1}
$$

Ten chain-aware theorems control forty balanced entries. Another 848
balanced entries remain unresolved: eight carry physical lower-witness
substitutions, four carry local-slice substitutions, and 836 retain the
provisional \(1/q\) target. The tiny margin means that sequential local
repairs are unlikely to be decisive unless they are substantially below
their reoptimized admissible gates.

## Deliverable 1 — authoritative ledger reconstruction

Build one generated table with one row per physical orbit/split containing:

- degree and block profile;
- row/column record data;
- occurrence multiplicity;
- exact attenuation and promise factor;
- current theorem coefficient or interval;
- current provisional coefficient;
- shared-law compatibility class;
- contribution to the total;
- proof and regression source; and
- status label.

The generated total must reproduce every published partial and diagnostic
number from exact or interval arithmetic.

Status: the generated classification and floating-point reconstruction are
complete. Outward interval certification remains part of Deliverable 4.
See notes/FINITE_SIZE_LEDGER_AUDIT.md.

## Deliverable 2 — compatible lower-witness vector

Use one physical law across the leading 16 unresolved orbits, which carry
51.1 percent of the current unresolved Perron contribution. Extend to the
leading 51 orbits, carrying 90.5 percent, if the first stage is inconclusive.
For every covered family compute:

- its total provisional charge;
- its admissible coefficient before the \(1/3\) gate closes;
- the best known row, column, Gram, and chain contractions;
- whether a matching physical lower witness exists; and
- its coefficient under the same latent law; and
- the reoptimized ledger value for the whole compatible vector.

The lower bounds must come from one jointly realizable law. Independently
optimized witnesses cannot be combined.

If this vector forces the scalar ledger to at least \(1/3\), record a
certified obstruction to the independent scalar architecture and pivot. The
result does not by itself refute the hard instance or construct a passive
protocol.

## Deliverable 3 — shared upper contraction

If the compatible lower-witness vector stays below \(1/3\), prove one joint
contraction over at least the leading 51 orbits, or an equivalent natural
family carrying at least 90 percent of unresolved Perron contribution.

The contraction must retain common-law compatibility and cross-cut
cancellation. Retain the witness only if the one-batch bound is rigorous or
interval-certifiable and leaves at least \(10^{-3}\) reserve, absent a proved
adaptive estimate supporting a different threshold.

Status: the occupation-side factorization is complete. The target is one
241-edge kernel on 125 states with eight connected components, generated by
six unordered profile patterns. The configuration-level shared contraction
is still open. See notes/SHARED_FRONTIER_STRUCTURE.md.

## Deliverable 4 — pass or fail certificate

One of the following must be produced.

### Pass

A rational or outward-rounded interval certificate proving the exact
conditioned one-batch transcript upper bound is below \(1/3\), with every
entry linked to a theorem.

### Fail

A certified physical coefficient, compatible family, or actual one-batch
protocol showing that the proposed completion cannot fit below \(1/3\).
State whether the failure is in the hard instance, the ledger architecture,
or only one candidate contraction.

### Inconclusive

Allowed only with a quantified interval and a named missing theorem whose
maximum possible effect is explicit.  An inconclusive outcome triggers the
hard-instance and passive-protocol tracks rather than another unranked local
calculation.

## Deliverable 5 — finite-size sweep

Implement the certified ledger as a function of \(N\) and evaluate

$$
N=256,1024,4096.
$$

For each size output \(M=4N\), the complete passive bound, the margin to
\(1/3\), promise-conditioning loss, and certification status.  If \(N=1024\)
fails, locate the first passing power of two before doing further local
algebra.

The omitted values \(512\) and \(2048\) are not squares \(q^2\) and therefore
are unsupported by the current signed-permutation implementation. They may
re-enter with a rectangular or replacement witness.

## Deliverable 6 — paper resource row

For the smallest passing \(N_*\), complete the corresponding row in
EXPERIMENTAL_SCORECARD.md and PAPER_TARGET.md.  The result is paper-ready
only when an experimental collaborator can identify the mode count,
traversals, state preparation, receiver, and uncovered idealizations.

## Stop rules

Stop this route when:

- a compatible certified lower-witness vector forces the scalar ledger to
  at least \(1/3\);
- no shared contraction covers 90 percent of the unresolved impact with the
  predeclared reserve;
- the same shared-law obstruction recurs across the leading families; or
- an alternative hard instance has a strictly better certified scorecard.

## Successor

If the one-batch gate passes at a credible size, the next project is the
adaptive posterior-selection lift and resource translation.  If it fails
throughout the feasibility window, the next project is an explicit
replacement hard instance informed by the failed family.
