# Fixed-pair adjacent-row contraction at q=64

## Result

The four-entry orbit generated by

$$
(1,1,3,5):(0,1,2,2)
$$

has arbitrary-diagonal one-batch coefficient

$$
\boxed{0.0699895941730}.
$$

This raises the theorem count from 260 to 264 and leaves 624 balanced
entries open. The optimized routing total improves to

$$
0.329248254134,
$$

with margin $0.00408507919913$ below one third.

More importantly, retaining this theorem and assigning the inherited local
slice scales to all 164 still-open quintic entries gives diagnostic total

$$
0.331066966509.
$$

Its margin is $0.00226636682480$, leaving $0.00126636682480$ after the
declared $0.001$ rounding and proof allowance. Thus the entire remaining
quintic proxy now fits with visible reserve. Those 164 proxy coefficients
are not yet theorems.

## Complete-row reduction

Write the cubic support as $C=E\mathbin{\dot\cup}\{x\}$ and the quintic
support as $S=F\mathbin{\dot\cup}G$, with $|E|=|F|=2$ and $|G|=3$. Rows
are $(b,E,F)$, columns are $(a,x,G)$, and

$$
K_{(b,E,F),(a,x,G)}
=H(a,b)M_{13}(b,C)M_{35}(C,S).
$$

For each row, use the complete vector

$$
\left(M_{13}(b,C)M_{35}(C,S)\right)_{x,G}
$$

as one Schur feature. After removing it, duplicate compression reduces the
remaining repeated Hadamard matrix to a weighted Hadamard block of trace
norm at most the geometric mean of the two total masses. The desired
coefficient is therefore the square root of the largest complete-row
energy.

## Why the row energy is small

For a fixed cubic pair $E$, the nonzero completions divide into three
classes:

| class | maximum count | squared $M_{13}$ amplitude |
|---|---:|---:|
| L-shaped record one | $2(q-1)$ | $1/[q^2(q-1)^2]$ |
| horizontal record three | $q-2$ | $1/q^2$ |
| remaining record three | $(q-1)(q-2)$ | $1/[q^2(q-1)^2]$ |

The first two counts are only linear in $q$. This is the gain lost by the
older product of independent worst slices.

The inherited fixed-pair $M_{35}$ tail bounds at $q=64$ are

$$
T_1=36.3949893507,
$$

and

$$
T_3=\max\{0.300219082874,0.0124564037672\}
=0.300219082874.
$$

Consequently

$$
R_1
=\frac{2(q-1)T_1}{q^2(q-1)^2}
=0.000282079220538,
$$

and

$$
R_3
=T_3\left(
\frac{q-2}{q^2}
+\frac{(q-1)(q-2)}{q^2(q-1)^2}
\right)
=0.00461646407197.
$$

Thus

$$
\sqrt{R_1+R_3}=0.0699895941730.
$$

Complement and reversal give the same theorem on the other three entries.

## Stress tests and scope

Complete $q=4$ enumeration gives maximum exact row energy
$0.465663580247$, below the analytic $q=4$ majorant. Exhaustive $q=4$ and
$q=8$ completion classifications reproduce all three counts and endpoint
amplitudes above.

This result is an arbitrary-law theorem for the floating one-batch ledger.
It is not an outward-rounded certificate, does not prove the other 164
quintic proxy values, and does not address the adaptive lift.
