## 4. Invariant correlation stress test

The inherited physical witness samples the two nonzero vertical pair
differences independently. Under the full row-label linear symmetry, an
ordered pair of nonzero differences has two orbits: equal and distinct.
`searches/opposite_endpoint_correlated_mixture.py` optimizes the mass on the
equal orbit while keeping the same uniform one-column triple law.

The independent value has equal-orbit mass \(1/31\) and coefficient
\(0.039593955294628\). The optimized invariant family gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
p_{\rm equal}&=0.019251519500609,\\
c_{\rm invariant}&=0.039599649575373.
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.1}
$$

This is a valid physical lower witness, but it remains below (3.3) by
(0.001862668721142).

The test is deliberately restricted to symmetry-invariant laws. A previous
exploratory use of the fourteen-class reduction on a nonsymmetric law was
rejected by the direct (q=4) comparison and is not part of the result.

## 5. Finite-size geometry correction

The signed-permutation implementation uses \(N=q^2\) with power-of-two
\(q\). In the initially declared window, the current witness geometry
therefore supports only

$$
N\in\{256,1024,4096\},
\qquad q\in\{16,32,64\}.
\tag{5.1}
$$

\(N=512\) and \(2048\) remain legitimate task sizes, but not for the current
square-order signed-permutation implementation without a new rectangular
construction. The full low-sector, promise, and physical-orbit ledger is
currently calibrated only at \(N=1024\).

## 6. Decision

The opposite-endpoint gate (3.3) is too narrow a project by itself. Even a
successful four-entry theorem would leave 844 entries in 223 other unresolved
orbits. The joint impact frontier is now the primary target:

- the leading 16 unresolved orbits carry 51.1 percent of the current
  unresolved Perron contribution; and
- the leading 51 carry 90.5 percent.

First optimize one compatible physical law across that frontier and insert
the induced coefficient vector into the reoptimized scalar ledger. A
certified total at least \(1/3\) closes the independent scalar architecture
and triggers the hard-instance pivot. It does not prove a passive protocol or
refute the hard instance.

If the compatible vector stays below \(1/3\), the only acceptable
continuation is a shared-law contraction covering at least the leading 51
orbits, or an equivalent family carrying 90 percent of unresolved impact,
with visible reserve. Independent orbit-by-orbit closure is no longer the
Round 4 strategy. See JOINT_DECISION_TARGET.md.
