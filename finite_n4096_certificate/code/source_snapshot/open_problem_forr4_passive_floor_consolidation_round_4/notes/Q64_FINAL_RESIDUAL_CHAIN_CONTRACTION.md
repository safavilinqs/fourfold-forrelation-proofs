## 1. Physical occurrence matrices

Let $q=2^m\ge8$, let $\Omega=\mathbb F_2^m\times\mathbb F_2^m$, and let $M(S,T)$ be the exact signed-permutation link moment. For a profile $d=(d_0,d_1,d_2,d_3)$ and a balanced split $t$, write each simple full support as

$$
U_i=A_i\sqcup B_i,
\qquad
|A_i|=t_i,
\qquad
|B_i|=d_i-t_i.
$$

The disjoint union is the physical cross-cut distinctness mask. The occurrence kernel is

$$
\mathcal O_t(A,B)
=
\mathbf 1\{A_i\cap B_i=\varnothing\text{ for every }i\}
\prod_{j=0}^2M(U_j,U_{j+1}).
$$

The row law $p(A)$ and column law $w(B)$ may each correlate every block variable. The claimed coefficient bounds

$$
\left\|
\operatorname{diag}(\sqrt p)\,
\mathcal O_t\,
\operatorname{diag}(\sqrt w)
\right\|_1
$$

uniformly over all such laws. No product law, uniform law, exchangeability, or translation-invariance hypothesis is used.

Every central record is the cardinality of the odd row or column set on its link. Record sectors are disjoint physical support families. When a complete scalar row has squared energy $E$, its entries form a row feature of norm at most $\sqrt E$, while the completion label is a unit column feature. Weighted Hilbert--Schmidt factorization therefore gives trace norm at most $\sqrt E$ for arbitrary $p,w$. Different record sectors occupy orthogonal feature coordinates, so their squared row energies may be added before the final square root. Every unused completed link is retained only as a unit cross-Gram Schur multiplier.

Complementing a split transposes the physical occurrence matrix. Reversing the path transposes every support and reverses the record sequence. Both operations preserve simple supports and every cross-cut mask, so each canonical calculation proves its full four-entry orbit.
