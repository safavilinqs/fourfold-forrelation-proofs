\title{A Polynomial Passive-Dose Lower Bound for Fourfold Forrelation}
\author{Amir H. Safavi-Naeini}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We give a standalone derivation of a polynomial lower bound for deciding the exact two-sided
fourfold-forrelation promise by adaptive fresh passive optical batches.
A batch may use an arbitrary mixed bosonic state whose sign-mode photon
number is at most its charge on the entire support, arbitrary bypass
idlers, one direct-sum sign-mask interaction, and an arbitrary collective
measurement.  Between batches only a classical outcome is retained.
For every power of two \(N\ge 2^{30}\), worst-case error at most \(1/3\)
implies
\[
        D\ge \frac{2}{15}N^{1/8}.
\]
The exponent is a fixed unconditional positive constant; it does not meet
the original stronger target \(\alpha>1/4\).
The argument uses a fresh-pivot inequality that controls arbitrary
fixed-level Fourier functionals of the full adaptive protocol, including
all cross-batch symmetric-difference cancellations.  A correlated
Gaussian sign construction then supplies signed Fourier moments of order
\(N^{-3r/8}\) at level \(r\), while a separate concentration argument
moves the average-case construction to the exact promises
\(\Forr\ge 1/4\) and \(\Forr\le-1/4\).
\end{abstract}

\section{Problem statement}

Fix an integer \(d\ge1\), put \(N=2^d\), and identify \([N]\) with
\(\F_2^d\).  Let
\[
 H_{ij}=N^{-1/2}(-1)^{\langle i,j\rangle},
 \qquad
 |u\rangle=N^{-1/2}\sum_i|i\rangle .
\]
For
\[
 x=(x^{(1)},x^{(2)},x^{(3)},x^{(4)}),
 \qquad x^{(b)}\in\{\pm1\}^N,
\]
write \(D_b=\diag(x^{(b)})\) and define
\[
 \Forr(x)=
 \langle u|D_1HD_2HD_3HD_4|u\rangle .
\]
Equivalently, with \(h_{ij}=(-1)^{\langle i,j\rangle}\),
\[
 \Forr(x)=
 N^{-5/2}\sum_{i,j,k,l}
 h_{ij}h_{jk}h_{kl}
 x^{(1)}_i x^{(2)}_j x^{(3)}_k x^{(4)}_l .
\]
The exact promises are
\[
 \Pi_+=\{x:\Forr(x)\ge1/4\},
 \qquad
 \Pi_-=\{x:\Forr(x)\le-1/4\}.
\]

A passive batch of charge \(t\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\) prepares an arbitrary, possibly mixed,
bosonic state on the \(4N\) sign modes and arbitrary bypass idlers,
supported on the sector in which the total sign-mode photon number is at
most \(t\).  It applies exactly once
\[
 \Gamma\left(
 (D_1\oplus D_2\oplus D_3\oplus D_4)\oplus I_{\rm idler}
 \right)
\]
and then performs an arbitrary collective POVM with a finite or
standard-Borel outcome space.  Number superpositions, vacuum coherence,
repeated mode occupation, bosonic symmetrization, arbitrary idlers,
heralding, singular outcomes, and postselection are allowed; the charge
is the maximum sign-mode photon number on the support, not its
expectation.

A passive protocol is an arbitrary adaptive tree of fresh batches.  Only
the classical outcome and private classical randomness cross a node
boundary; no quantum state or quantum memory does.  The next state,
charge, POVM, stopping rule, and output may depend on the full classical
history.  If the charge at history \(h\) is \(t_h\), the hard dose is
\[
 D=\sup_{\text{maximal branches }\gamma}
   \sum_{h\in\gamma}t_h,
\]
where the sum on an infinite branch means the supremum over its finite
prefixes.  Whenever $D<\infty$, it is a nonnegative integer because it
is a bounded supremum of integer-valued branch-prefix charges.  Every
traversal is charged, including failed, heralded, controlled, inverse, or
conditioned traversals; conditioning never discounts dose.  A zero-charge
continuation is input-independent and may be absorbed into classical
randomness, with nontermination treated as a terminal outcome.  The
protocol decides the promise with error at most \(1/3\) when it outputs
the correct sign with probability at least \(2/3\) for every individual
input in \(\Pi_+\cup\Pi_-\).

\begin{theorem}[Main theorem]\label{thm:main}
Let
\[
 \alpha=\frac18,\qquad c_0=\frac{2}{15},\qquad N_0=2^{30}.
\]
For every power of two \(N\ge N_0\), every passive protocol in the full
model above that decides \(\Pi_+\) versus \(\Pi_-\) with worst-case error
at most \(1/3\) satisfies
\[
       D\ge c_0N^\alpha.
\]
\end{theorem}

\section{Walsh notation and the bosonic slot embedding}

Put \(M=4N\) and identify the sign coordinates with \([M]\).  Thus \(N\)
is the number of coordinates on each of the four masks, while \(M\) is only
temporary notation for the total number of Boolean sign coordinates.  For
\(Q\subseteq[M]\), write
\[
 \chi_Q(x)=\prod_{a\in Q}x_a,
 \qquad
 \wh f(Q)=2^{-M}\sum_{x\in\{\pm1\}^M}f(x)\chi_Q(x).
\]

\subsection{The exact labeled-slot embedding}

Let
\[
 \cQ=\mathbb C|\bot\rangle\oplus\mathbb C^M,
\]
where \(|\bot\rangle\) is a vacuum symbol, and define
\[
 O_x|\bot\rangle=|\bot\rangle,
 \qquad
 O_x|a\rangle=x_a|a\rangle
 \quad(a\in[M]).
\]
For every \(t\), the truncated sign-mode Fock space
\[
 \bigoplus_{n=0}^t\Sym^n(\mathbb C^M)
\]
embeds isometrically into \(\cQ^{\otimes t}\): an \(n\)-photon symmetric
vector is mapped to the symmetric \(t\)-slot vector with \(t-n\) vacuum
symbols.  Under this embedding, the second-quantized sign mask is exactly
\(O_x^{\otimes t}\).  Hence a charge-\(t\) batch is an arbitrary
\(t\)-slot parallel phase query with arbitrary ancilla.

For
\(\bm a=(a_1,\ldots,a_t)\in(\{\bot\}\cup[M])^t\), define
\[
 \partial\bm a
 =
 \left\{a\in[M]:
 \#\{s:a_s=a\}\ \text{is odd}\right\}.
\]
Then
\[
 O_x^{\otimes t}|\bm a\rangle
 =\chi_{\partial\bm a}(x)|\bm a\rangle .
\]
This representation retains repeated occupation, bosonic symmetrization,
number superpositions, and vacuum coherence.  An arbitrary purification
and every bypass idler are included in an additional ancilla factor.

\section{Finite fresh trees}

We first treat a finite tree with finite outcome sets.  Purify every
prepared state.  At a nonterminal history \(h\), let \(t_h\) be the
charge, let
\[
 |\psi_h\rangle\in\cQ^{\otimes t_h}\otimes\cA_h
\]
be the prepared state, and let
\(\{M_{h,y}:y\in Y_h\}\) be the POVM.  Use the square-root instrument
\[
 W_{h,y}=M_{h,y}^{1/2},
 \qquad
 \sum_{y\in Y_h}W_{h,y}^*W_{h,y}=I.
\]
At a terminal history \(h\), let \(a_h\in[0,1]\) be the conditional
probability of outputting \(+\).

Define recursively a vector-valued accepted amplitude.  At a terminal
node, put
\[
 \Phi_h(x)=\sqrt{a_h}\in\mathbb C.
\]
At a nonterminal node, put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:accepted-amplitude}
 \Phi_h(x)
 =
 \bigoplus_{y\in Y_h}
 \left(
 W_{h,y}O_x^{\otimes t_h}|\psi_h\rangle
 \right)
 \otimes\Phi_{hy}(x).
\end{equation}
The tensor product in \eqref{eq:accepted-amplitude} records the fresh
boundary: the child protocol acts on a newly prepared system and never
acts on the residual output system of the parent batch.  In the pivot
proof this is used both to construct a genuine separating cut through all
marked parallel slots and to factor every continuation as an operator on
a fresh tensor factor.  Directly,
\[
 \norm{\Phi_h(x)}^2
 =
 \sum_y p_h(y|x)\norm{\Phi_{hy}(x)}^2.
\]
Thus, at the root,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:f-norm}
       f(x)=\norm{\Phi_\varnothing(x)}^2,
\end{equation}
where \(f(x)\) is the probability of outputting \(+\).

Along a branch, the total number of query slots is at most \(D\).
Consequently \(\Phi_\varnothing\) is a vector-valued Walsh polynomial of
degree at most \(D\), and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:degree}
       \wh f(Q)=0\qquad(|Q|>2D).
\end{equation}

\section{The fresh-pivot inequality}

The next theorem is the structural core of the proof.

\begin{theorem}[Fresh-pivot inequality]\label{thm:pivot}
Let \(f\) be the acceptance probability of a passive protocol of hard
dose at most \(D\).  If \(1\le r\le2D\) and
\(\{c_Q:Q\subseteq[M], |Q|=r\}\) is any family of complex numbers, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pivot}
 \left|
 \sum_{|Q|=r}c_Q\wh f(Q)
 \right|
 \le
 \left(
   \sum_{k=0}^{r}
   \binom Dk\binom D{r-k}M^{\min(k,r-k)/2}
 \right)
 \max_{|Q|=r}|c_Q|
 \le
 \binom{2D}{r}M^{r/4}
 \max_{|Q|=r}|c_Q|.
\end{equation}
The estimate holds for arbitrary standard-Borel outcomes, singular
histories, unrestricted tree width, private randomness, and infinite
zero-charge tails.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Last-occurrence records}

Expanding \eqref{eq:accepted-amplitude} in all labeled slot bases gives
\[
 \Phi_\varnothing(x)
 =
 \sum_\gamma
 \chi_{\partial\gamma}(x)v_\gamma,
\]
where \(\gamma\) ranges over complete ket execution histories, including
the classical terminal path and every slot word, and
\(\partial\gamma\) is the symmetric difference of the slot parities along
that history.  Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fourier-history}
 \wh f(Q)
 =
 \sum_{\substack{\gamma,\gamma'\\
 \partial\gamma\triangle\partial\gamma'=Q}}
 \langle v_{\gamma'},v_\gamma\rangle .
\end{equation}
The direct-sum outcome registers make the inner product zero unless the
two histories have the same classical terminal path, while the tensor
products retain the fresh factorization along that path.

Pad every ket execution and every bra execution by vacuum slots so that
each has exactly \(D\) slot positions, ordered chronologically within its
own side.

A doubled slot history contributes to \(\wh f(Q)\) exactly when the ket
and bra multiplicities have different parity precisely on \(Q\).  Thus,
for every \(a\in Q\), exactly one of the two sides contains \(a\) an odd
number of times.  On that odd-parity side, mark the last occurrence of
\(a\).  These \(r=|Q|\) marked positions are distinct.  Their set
\[
       R\subseteq
       \bigl(\{\mathrm{ket}\}\times[D]\bigr)
       \sqcup
       \bigl(\{\mathrm{bra}\}\times[D]\bigr),
       \qquad |R|=r,
\]
is the parity-side last-occurrence record.  Every surviving doubled
history has one and only one record, so the number of possible records is
at most \(\binom{2D}{r}\) (empty record classes do not matter).

For a fixed record, let \(L\subseteq R\) be the marked ket positions and
let \(R\setminus L\) be the marked bra positions.  If \(k=|L|\), the
coordinate values at the marked ket positions form a tuple
\(\alpha\in[M]^k\), and those at the marked bra positions form
\(\beta\in[M]^{r-k}\).  A combined tuple \((\alpha,\beta)\) with any
repeated coordinate value is declared inadmissible.

\subsection{Parity tracking and exact record filters}

Let
\[
 \cK=\ell_2(\mathcal P([M]))
\]
with basis \(|S\rangle\), \(S\subseteq[M]\).  For \(T\subseteq[M]\), let
\[
 X_T|S\rangle=|S\triangle T\rangle.
\]
At every unmarked query slot, the coordinate value toggles the parity
register.  At a marked slot, the value is exposed as an open coordinate
index and its toggle is omitted.  If the exposed ket tuple is
\(\alpha=(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_k)\), write
\[
 A(\alpha)=\{\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_k\},
 \qquad
 \Pi^{\rm res}_{\alpha}
 =\sum_{S\cap A(\alpha)=\varnothing}|S\rangle\langle S|,
\]
and define \(B(\beta)\) and \(\Pi^{\rm res}_{\beta}\) analogously.
The ket side is projected at the terminal cut by
\(\Pi^{\rm res}_{\alpha}\), the bra side by
\(\Pi^{\rm res}_{\beta}\), and the two residual parity registers are
then identified by the identity map on \(\cK\).

The last-occurrence condition is imposed separately on each side.  Once a
coordinate has been exposed at a marked slot, every later slot on that
same side is projected away from that coordinate.  These restrictions and
the terminal residual-parity restrictions are controlled orthogonal
projections internal to one side.  No row-index-controlled projection acts
on the bra side, and no column-index-controlled projection acts on the ket
side.

These filters are equivalent to the record definition.  Indeed, for an
exposed ket coordinate \(a\in A(\alpha)\), omission of the marked
occurrence and the projection
\(\Pi^{\rm res}_{\alpha}\) make the remaining ket multiplicity of \(a\)
even.  Hence the full ket multiplicity is odd.  Equality of the two
residual parity sets makes the bra multiplicity even.  The argument for an
exposed bra coordinate is symmetric.  Every unexposed coordinate has the
same parity on the two sides.  The no-later-occurrence projections make
the designated occurrence the last one on its odd-parity side.
Consequently every surviving doubled history is admitted by exactly its
true parity-side last-occurrence record and by no other record.

\begin{lemma}[Fresh cut contraction]\label{lem:cut}
Fix a record \(R\) and its ket/bra split.  The total contribution of all
doubled histories with this record can be written
\begin{equation}\label{eq:block-form}
 \sum_{\alpha,\beta}
 C_{\alpha\beta}
 \langle u_\alpha,E_{\alpha\beta}v_\beta\rangle ,
\end{equation}
where
\[
 \sum_\alpha\norm{u_\alpha}^2\le1,
 \qquad
 \sum_\beta\norm{v_\beta}^2\le1,
\]
and the block operator
\[
 E=[E_{\alpha\beta}]:
 \bigoplus_\beta\cV_\beta
 \longrightarrow
 \bigoplus_\alpha\cU_\alpha
\]
satisfies \(\norm E\le1\).  The scalar entry is
\[
 C_{\alpha\beta}
 =
 \begin{cases}
 c_Q,&\text{if all entries of }(\alpha,\beta)\text{ are distinct and form }Q,\\
 0,&\text{otherwise.}
 \end{cases}
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
We construct the vectors and the operator by cutting the finite doubled
execution network at the marked coordinate resolutions.

For a slot \(q\), let \(P^{(q)}_a\) be the coordinate projector on that
slot, with \(a\in\{\bot\}\cup[M]\), and put
\(\epsilon(\bot)=\varnothing\),
\(\epsilon(a)=\{a\}\) for \(a\in[M]\).  The unmarked slot map on the
slot and parity register is
\[
 \mathsf T_q
 =\sum_{a\in\{\bot\}\cup[M]}
 P^{(q)}_a\otimes X_{\epsilon(a)}.
\]
It is unitary.  At a marked slot we replace this map by the coordinate
extraction
\[
 \mathsf R_q\xi
 =\bigoplus_{a\in[M]}P^{(q)}_a\xi .
\]
The summands are mutually orthogonal, so \(\mathsf R_q\) is a
contraction; its omission of the vacuum summand is harmless because a
marked value belongs to \(Q\subseteq[M]\).  The direct sum here is the
canonical orthogonal decomposition of the actual slot space into the
ranges of the coordinate projectors; it is not a collection of freely
copied label spaces.  Because all slots in one batch are parallel, the
chosen cut can pass immediately after every marked extraction in that
batch.  Products of the extraction maps then give mutually orthogonal
joint row projections \(P^{\rm ket}_\alpha\) and joint column
projections \(P^{\rm bra}_\beta\) on the physical cut spaces.
These are orthogonal ranges at the cut; an outcome-dependent continuation
need not preserve their orthogonality after it is pushed to the terminal
boundary, which is why the intervening block contraction below cannot be
removed.

At a node \(h\), write
\[
 \mathsf W_h\xi=\bigoplus_{y\in Y_h}W_{h,y}\xi .
\]
This is an isometry.  The preparation map
\(z\mapsto z\otimes\psi_h\), the unmarked toggles, the extraction maps,
the controlled no-later-occurrence projections, and the controlled
terminal projections \(\Pi^{\rm res}_{\alpha}\) or
\(\Pi^{\rm res}_{\beta}\) are therefore all contractions.

For reference, put
\[
 P_{\bm a}=\left(\bigotimes_{q=1}^{t_h}P^{(q)}_{a_q}\right)\otimes I_{\cA_h}.
\]
When the local batch contains no marked slot, the resulting map on the
incoming parity register is the explicitly defined isometry
\begin{equation}\label{eq:J-map}
 J_h|S\rangle
 =\bigoplus_{y\in Y_h}
   \sum_{\bm a}
   |S\triangle\partial\bm a\rangle
   \otimes W_{h,y}P_{\bm a}|\psi_h\rangle .
\end{equation}
Indeed, for \(S,T\subseteq[M]\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \langle J_hT,J_hS\rangle
 &=\sum_{y,\bm a,\bm b}
   \mathbf 1_{\{T\triangle\partial\bm b
                  =S\triangle\partial\bm a\}}
   \langle P_{\bm b}\psi_h,
           M_{h,y}P_{\bm a}\psi_h\rangle\\
 &=\sum_{\bm a,\bm b}
   \mathbf 1_{\{T\triangle\partial\bm b
                  =S\triangle\partial\bm a\}}
   \langle P_{\bm b}\psi_h,P_{\bm a}\psi_h\rangle\\
 &=\mathbf 1_{\{S=T\}}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The marked local maps are obtained from \eqref{eq:J-map} by replacing the
appropriate toggles by the extraction maps \(\mathsf R_q\), retaining the
extracted values as open orthogonal range factors, and composing the
same-side controlled projections described above.  The cut-edge spaces
are defined recursively.  At a terminal history they consist of the
residual parity wire.  At an internal history \(h\), the ket cut-edge
space is the direct sum, over \(y\in Y_h\), of tensor products of the
parent residual system \(\cE_{h,y}\), the ket cut-edge space of the
child \(hy\), and the orthogonal range factors created by any local ket
extractions; the bra space is defined in the same way.  We denote these spaces by
\(\cU_h\) and \(\cV_h\).

Contracting the local ket tensors from the entrance of the batch to these
cut edges defines
\[
 A_{h,{\rm k}}:\cU_h^{\rm in}
 \longrightarrow
 \bigoplus_{y\in Y_h}\cE_{h,y}\otimes\cU_{hy},
\]
and contracting the corresponding bra tensors defines
\[
 A_{h,{\rm b}}:\cV_h^{\rm in}
 \longrightarrow
 \bigoplus_{y\in Y_h}\cE_{h,y}\otimes\cV_{hy}.
\]
Here the harmless notation \(\cU_h^{\rm in},\cV_h^{\rm in}\) denotes the
wires entering the local portion of the chosen cut; local extraction
range factors are included on the appropriate side of these maps.  Thus
these maps are completely specified by the preparation injection, the
slot maps \(\mathsf T_q\) and \(\mathsf R_q\), the instrument isometry,
and the controlled record projections.  The construction above gives
\[
 \norm{A_{h,{\rm k}}}\le1,
 \qquad
 \norm{A_{h,{\rm b}}}\le1.
\]

Choose a zig-zag cut through the doubled acyclic network that intersects
each marked ket slot immediately after its ket extraction and each marked
bra slot immediately after its bra extraction.  This is where the fresh
architecture first becomes load bearing.  The \(t_h\) query slots inside
one batch are parallel, and no quantum-memory wire passes from a parent
batch to a child batch, so the cut is genuinely separating and never
crosses one persistent quantum wire twice.  In a coherent serial-query
circuit, marks at different query times lie on the same evolving wire and
no analogous family of simultaneous physical cut projections exists.  The cut edges are actual
physical slot, environment, open-coordinate, and parity wires.  Contracting
all one-sided tensors exterior to these cuts, starting with the prepared
states and the empty parity register, produces
\[
 u=\bigoplus_\alpha u_\alpha,
 \qquad
 v=\bigoplus_\beta v_\beta .
\]
The extraction ranges are orthogonal and every one-sided tensor is a
contraction, hence
\[
 \norm u^2=\sum_\alpha\norm{u_\alpha}^2\le1,
 \qquad
 \norm v^2=\sum_\beta\norm{v_\beta}^2\le1.
\]

The tensors remaining between the two sides of the cut define one operator
\(E:\bigoplus_\beta\cV_\beta\to
      \bigoplus_\alpha\cU_\alpha\).  The spaces
\(\cU_\alpha\) and \(\cV_\beta\) are the ranges of the joint physical
cut projections, not abstract copies of one common space.
This operator is defined recursively, not entry by entry.  At a terminal
history \(h\), it is the scalar acceptance factor \(a_h\) times the
identity identification of the two residual parity registers, with the
same-side residual projections already included in the side maps.  At an
internal history it is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pullback}
 E_h
 =A_{h,{\rm k}}^*
   \left(
     \bigoplus_{y\in Y_h}
     I_{\cE_{h,y}}\otimes E_{hy}
   \right)
   A_{h,{\rm b}}.
\end{equation}
The direct-sum decomposition records the common classical outcome.  The
factor \(I_{\cE_{h,y}}\otimes E_{hy}\) is the second load-bearing use
of freshness: the child acts on a newly prepared system and never acts on
the residual quantum system of the parent batch.  Together with the
separating-cut property above, this is what distinguishes the proof from
a coherent serial-query calculation.  Backward induction gives
\[
 \norm{E_h}\le1
\]
at every history.  In particular \(\norm E\le1\), and its blocks are
\[
 E_{\alpha\beta}
 =P^{\rm ket}_\alpha E P^{\rm bra}_\beta .
\]
For a single batch this construction is the familiar formula
\[
 u_\alpha=P^{\rm ket}_\alpha\psi,
 \qquad
 v_\beta=P^{\rm bra}_\beta\psi,
 \qquad
 E=\sum_y a_yM_y,
\]
so the block family is obtained by compressing one positive contraction,
not by pairing unrelated vectors through an entrywise identity.

Expanding the maps \(A_{h,{\rm k}}\), \(A_{h,{\rm b}}\), and
\eqref{eq:pullback} in the slot bases reproduces exactly the products of
local matrix elements in \eqref{eq:fourier-history}.  The parity identity,
the no-later-occurrence projections, and the residual-disjointness
projections select exactly the histories with the fixed record, as proved
above.  Multiplication by \(C_{\alpha\beta}\) inserts the coefficient
\(c_Q\) for the distinct exposed set \(Q\).  This proves
\eqref{eq:block-form} with the stated vectors and the stated single block
contraction.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why the middle contraction cannot be discarded]\label{rem:not-identity}
It is tempting to push the complete measured continuation into the side
vectors and replace \(E\) by the identity.  That is false even for one
batch.  Take a two-dimensional query space with coordinate basis
\(|1\rangle,|2\rangle\), prepare
\(\psi=(|1\rangle+|2\rangle)/\sqrt2\), measure in the
\(|+\rangle,|-\rangle\) basis, and accept only the \(+\) outcome.  The
full instrument is an isometry, but the accepted terminal vectors
associated with the physical inputs \(|1\rangle\) and \(|2\rangle\) are
both proportional to \(|+\rangle\) and are not orthogonal.  The correct
single-batch representation keeps the orthogonal vectors
\(u_a=P_a\psi\) at the physical cut and places the outcome-dependent
acceptance in the contraction
\(E=\sum_y a_yM_y\).  The general construction in
Lemma~\ref{lem:cut} is the adaptive version of exactly this separation.
\end{remark}

\subsection{A block Schur estimate}

For a scalar matrix \(C=(C_{\alpha\beta})\), define
\[
 \gamma_2(C)
 =
 \inf_{C_{\alpha\beta}=\langle p_\alpha,q_\beta\rangle}
 \left(\max_\alpha\norm{p_\alpha}\right)
 \left(\max_\beta\norm{q_\beta}\right).
\]

\begin{lemma}[Block Schur estimate]\label{lem:schur}
Under the hypotheses of Lemma~\ref{lem:cut},
\[
 \left|
 \sum_{\alpha,\beta}
 C_{\alpha\beta}
 \langle u_\alpha,E_{\alpha\beta}v_\beta\rangle
 \right|
 \le\gamma_2(C).
\]
If \(C\) has at most \(M^k\) rows, at most \(M^{r-k}\) columns, and
\(|C_{\alpha\beta}|\le c_*\), then
\[
 \gamma_2(C)
 \le c_*M^{\min(k,r-k)/2}
 \le c_*M^{r/4}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Choose a factorization
\(C_{\alpha\beta}=\langle p_\alpha,q_\beta\rangle\).
Tensor \(E\) with the factorization Hilbert space and apply it to the
direct-sum vectors
\[
 \bigoplus_\alpha p_\alpha\otimes u_\alpha,
 \qquad
 \bigoplus_\beta q_\beta\otimes v_\beta.
\]
The contraction of \(E\), Cauchy--Schwarz, and the two energy bounds prove
the first assertion.

For the second assertion, suppose first that \(M^k\le M^{r-k}\).  Take the
row factors to be the standard basis vectors and take the column factor
for a column of \(C\) to be that column.  The row norms are one and the
column norms are at most \(c_*M^{k/2}\).  If the number of columns is
smaller, transpose.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:pivot} for finite trees]
Uniform averaging against \(\chi_Q\) keeps exactly the doubled histories
whose total coordinate parity is \(Q\).  Partition those histories by
their unique last-occurrence record.  A record with \(k\) ket marks and
\(r-k\) bra marks has contribution at most
\[
 M^{\min(k,r-k)/2}\max_{|Q|=r}|c_Q|
\]
by Lemmas~\ref{lem:cut} and \ref{lem:schur}, and there are at most
\(\binom Dk\binom D{r-k}\) such records.  Summing over \(k\) gives the
first inequality in \eqref{eq:pivot}; Vandermonde's identity and
\(M^{\min(k,r-k)/2}\le M^{r/4}\) give the second.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Standard-Borel outcomes and infinite trees}

\begin{lemma}[Finite approximation]\label{lem:finite-approx}
For the finite input domain \(\{\pm1\}^M\), every standard-Borel fresh
protocol of hard dose at most \(D\) is a uniform limit of finite-outcome,
finite-width fresh protocols of hard dose at most \(D\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If \(D=0\), the entire protocol is input-independent and is already a
finite classical kernel up to an arbitrarily accurate finite
coarse-graining.  Assume henceforth that \(D\ge1\).

Every positive charge is an integer.  Hence a branch of dose at most
\(D\) contains at most \(D\) positive-charge nodes.  Collapse every
zero-charge subtree into its input-independent conditional kernel over
the next positive-charge history, a terminal outcome, or nontermination;
regard nontermination as a terminal outcome.

Proceed backward over the remaining positive-charge depth.  At a node
with standard-Borel outcome \(y\), let
\[
 g_y=(g_y(x))_{x\in\{\pm1\}^M}\in[0,1]^{2^M}
\]
be the continuation acceptance vector.  The map \(y\mapsto g_y\) is
measurable.  Partition the finite-dimensional cube into finitely many
Borel cells of supremum diameter at most \(\delta\).  Coarse-grain the
POVM by the inverse images of these cells and select one actual
continuation from each nonempty cell.  For every input, the change in
acceptance probability at that node is at most \(\delta\).  Iterating
through at most \(D\) positive-charge levels with
\(\delta=\varepsilon/D\) gives a uniform \(\varepsilon\) approximation
without increasing any branch charge.
\end{proof}

Every Fourier coefficient is continuous under uniform convergence.
Equation \eqref{eq:pivot} therefore passes to standard-Borel outcomes,
singular histories, unrestricted width, private randomness, and infinite
zero-charge tails.  Equation \eqref{eq:degree} passes to the limit as
well.

\section{The hard distributions}

Put
\[
       \rho=\frac{200}{201}.
\]
For \(s=1,2,3\), independently sample jointly Gaussian vectors
\[
 (Z_s,W_s)\in\mathbb R^N\times\mathbb R^N
\]
with
\[
 \E Z_sZ_s^{\mathsf T}=I,
 \qquad
 \E W_sW_s^{\mathsf T}=I,
 \qquad
 \E Z_sW_s^{\mathsf T}=\rho H.
\]
The block covariance is positive semidefinite because \(H\) is symmetric
orthogonal and \(0<\rho<1\).  Put
\[
 a_s=\operatorname{sgn}Z_s,
 \qquad
 b_s=\operatorname{sgn}W_s.
\]
Choose independent uniform gauges
\(\zeta_1,\zeta_2,\zeta_3\in\{\pm1\}\), set
\[
       \zeta_4=\sigma\zeta_1\zeta_2\zeta_3,
       \qquad \sigma\in\{\pm1\},
\]
and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hard}
\begin{aligned}
 x^{(1)}&=\zeta_1a_1,\\
 x^{(2)}&=\zeta_2(b_1\odot a_2),\\
 x^{(3)}&=\zeta_3(b_2\odot a_3),\\
 x^{(4)}&=\zeta_4b_3.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Let \(\mu_\sigma\) be the resulting law.

\section{Signed Fourier moments}

For \(A,B\subseteq[N]\), define
\[
 L(A,B)
 =
 \E\left[
 \prod_{i\in A}a_i
 \prod_{j\in B}b_j
 \right]
\]
for one Gaussian link.

\begin{lemma}[One-link moment bound]\label{lem:link}
Let \(a=|A|\), \(b=|B|\), and \(a+b\le r\le\sqrt N\).  Then
\[
       |L(A,B)|
       \le
       \left(\frac r{2\sqrt N}\right)^{\max(a,b)}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If both sets are empty, the assertion is immediate.  If exactly one of
\(A,B\) is empty, the moment is zero.  Assume \(a,b>0\).  The cross-covariance matrix between \(Z_A\) and \(W_B\) is
\[
 R=\rho H_{A,B},
 \qquad
 \norm R
 \le\norm R_{\mathrm F}
 =\rho\sqrt{\frac{ab}{N}}
 \le\frac{a+b}{2\sqrt N}
 \le\frac r{2\sqrt N},
 \qquad \norm R\le\rho<1.
\]
The function
\[
 F(Z_A)=\prod_{i\in A}\operatorname{sgn}Z_i
\]
is odd in each of its \(a\) coordinates, so its multivariate Hermite
expansion begins in total degree at least \(a\).  The analogous function
of \(W_B\) begins in total degree at least \(b\).

For jointly standard Gaussian vectors with cross-covariance \(R\), the
conditional-expectation operator between the two Gaussian \(L^2\) spaces
is the second quantization of \(R\).  On homogeneous chaos degree \(p\)
its operator norm is at most \(\norm R^p\).  If \(F_p,G_p\) are the
degree-\(p\) components and \(m=\max(a,b)\), then
\[
\begin{aligned}
 |L(A,B)|
 &\le
 \sum_{p\ge m}\norm R^p\norm{F_p}_2\norm{G_p}_2\\
 &\le
 \norm R^m
 \left(\sum_p\norm{F_p}_2^2\right)^{1/2}
 \left(\sum_p\norm{G_p}_2^2\right)^{1/2}\\
 &\le\norm R^m,
\end{aligned}
\]
because \(F\) and \(G\) are sign-valued.
\end{proof}

Let \(Q=(Q_1,Q_2,Q_3,Q_4)\), with \(Q_b\) contained in block \(b\).
Write
\[
 q_b=|Q_b|,
 \qquad
 r=q_1+q_2+q_3+q_4.
\]

\begin{lemma}[Signed Fourier moment]\label{lem:signed}
If \(r\le\sqrt N\), then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:signed-moment}
 \left|
 (\E_{\mu_+}-\E_{\mu_-})\chi_Q
 \right|
 \le
 2^{1-3r/4}r^{3r/4}N^{-3r/8}.
\end{equation}
The left-hand side is zero unless all four \(q_b\) are odd.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The gauge factor in \(\chi_Q(x)\) is
\[
 \sigma^{q_4}
 \zeta_1^{q_1+q_4}
 \zeta_2^{q_2+q_4}
 \zeta_3^{q_3+q_4}.
\]
Gauge averaging is therefore zero unless
\[
 q_1\equiv q_2\equiv q_3\equiv q_4\pmod2.
\]
The difference between \(\sigma=+1\) and \(\sigma=-1\) is zero unless the
common parity is odd.  In the surviving case,
\[
 (\E_{\mu_+}-\E_{\mu_-})\chi_Q
 =
 2L(Q_1,Q_2)L(Q_2,Q_3)L(Q_3,Q_4).
\]
Before coarsening by the total level, Lemma~\ref{lem:link} gives the
composition-dependent estimate
\[
 \left|(\E_{\mu_+}-\E_{\mu_-})\chi_Q\right|
 \le
 2\prod_{s=1}^{3}
 \left(
   \rho\sqrt{\frac{q_sq_{s+1}}{N}}
 \right)^{\max(q_s,q_{s+1})}.
\]
For nonnegative \(q_1,\ldots,q_4\),
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \max(q_1,q_2)&\ge\tfrac34q_1+\tfrac14q_2,\\
 \max(q_2,q_3)&\ge\tfrac12q_2+\tfrac12q_3,\\
 \max(q_3,q_4)&\ge\tfrac14q_3+\tfrac34q_4.
\end{aligned}
\]
The sum of the three right-hand sides is \(3r/4\).  Apply
Lemma~\ref{lem:link} to the three links.
\end{proof}

\section{Average-case indistinguishability}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:ind}
If a passive protocol has dose
\[
       D<\frac{2}{15}N^{1/8},
\]
then its acceptance probability \(f\) satisfies
\[
       \left|\E_{\mu_+}f-\E_{\mu_-}f\right|<\frac{14641}{109375}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By \eqref{eq:degree},
\[
 \E_{\mu_+}f-\E_{\mu_-}f
 =
 \sum_{r=0}^{2D}
 \sum_{|Q|=r}
 \wh f(Q)
 (\E_{\mu_+}-\E_{\mu_-})\chi_Q.
\]
Only \(r\ge4\) contributes.  Under the assumed dose bound,
\(2D<\sqrt N\), so Theorem~\ref{thm:pivot} and
Lemma~\ref{lem:signed} apply at every nonzero level:
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \left|\E_{\mu_+}f-\E_{\mu_-}f\right|
 &\le
 2\sum_{r=4}^{2D}
 \binom{2D}{r}
 (4N)^{r/4}
 2^{-3r/4}r^{3r/4}N^{-3r/8}\\
 &\le
 2\sum_{r=4}^{2D}
 \left(
 2^{3/4}eD\,r^{-1/4}N^{-1/8}
 \right)^r .
\end{aligned}
\]
Here we used \(\binom{2D}{r}\le(2eD/r)^r\).  For \(r\ge4\),
\[
 2^{3/4}r^{-1/4}
 =2\,2^{-1/4}r^{-1/4}
 \le 2\,8^{-1/4}.
\]
Thus the last display is at most
\[
 2\sum_{r=4}^{\infty}q^r
 =\frac{2q^4}{1-q},
 \qquad
 q=2eDN^{-1/8}8^{-1/4}.
\]
If \(D<2N^{1/8}/15\), then, using
\(e<11/4\) and \(8^{1/4}>5/3\),
\[
 q<\frac{11}{25}.
\]
Consequently
\[
 \frac{2q^4}{1-q}
 <
 \frac{2(11/25)^4}{1-11/25}
 =\frac{14641}{109375}.
\]
\end{proof}

\section{Mean and concentration of the hard distributions}

\subsection{Exact mean and exact normalization}

The Gaussian arcsine identity gives
\[
 \E[a_{s,i}b_{s,j}]
 =
 \frac2\pi\arcsin(\rho H_{ij})
 =
 \beta_NH_{ij},
\]
where
\[
 \beta_N
 =
 \frac{2\sqrt N}{\pi}
 \arcsin\left(\frac{\rho}{\sqrt N}\right).
\]
Using the exact \(N^{-5/2}\) coefficient and independence of the three
links,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mean}
       \E_{\mu_\sigma}\Forr(x)=\sigma\beta_N^3.
\end{equation}
Indeed, the three link moments contribute \(N^{-3/2}\), the defining
coefficient contributes \(N^{-5/2}\), and the four-index sum contributes
\(N^4\).

Since \(\arcsin z\ge z\), \(\rho=200/201\), and \(\pi<22/7\),
\[
       \beta_N>\frac{1400}{2211}.
\]
Therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:margin}
 \beta_N^3-\frac14
 >
 \left(\frac{1400}{2211}\right)^3-\frac14
 =
 \frac{167480069}{43234079724}
 >
 \frac1{260}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{A conditional-independent representation}

Every monomial in \(\Forr\) contains one coordinate from each block, and
\[
 \zeta_1\zeta_2\zeta_3\zeta_4=\sigma.
\]
Therefore the gauges contribute the deterministic overall factor
\(\sigma\) to \(\Forr\) and do not contribute to its variance.  We may
analyze the ungauged block vectors
\[
 a_1,\qquad b_1\odot a_2,\qquad b_2\odot a_3,\qquad b_3
\]
and restore the factor \(\sigma\) at the end.

Let \(G_s,\xi_s,\eta_s\) be independent standard Gaussian vectors and set
\[
 a_s=
 \operatorname{sgn}\left(G_s+\frac{\xi_s}{\sqrt{200}}\right),
 \qquad
 b_s=
 \operatorname{sgn}\left(HG_s+\frac{\eta_s}{\sqrt{200}}\right).
\]
After standardization, this pair has cross-covariance \(\rho H\), and its
signs have the same law as the preceding \(a_s,b_s\).  Conditional on
\(G_1,G_2,G_3\), the primitive signs
\(a_1,b_1,a_2,b_2,a_3,b_3\) are mutually independent across all
coordinates; hence the \(4N\) ungauged input coordinates displayed above
are mutually independent.  Moreover,
\[
 \E[a_{s,i}|G_s]=\phi(G_{s,i}),
 \qquad
 \E[b_{s,j}|G_s]=\phi((HG_s)_j),
\]
where
\[
       \phi(z)=\operatorname{erf}(10z),
       \qquad
       L:=\norm{\phi'}_\infty=\frac{20}{\sqrt\pi},
       \qquad
       L^2<128.
\]
The last inequality follows from \(\pi>25/8\).

Put
\[
       M(g)=D_{\phi(g)}HD_{\phi(Hg)}.
\]
Every \(M(g)\) is a contraction.  The conditional mean of \(\Forr\) is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Fbar}
 \overline F
 =
 \sigma\langle u|M(G_1)M(G_2)M(G_3)|u\rangle.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Conditional rounding variance}

Let \(\Forr^{\rm ung}\) denote fourfold forrelation evaluated on the
ungauged block vectors
\[
 y^{(1)}=a_1,\qquad
 y^{(2)}=b_1\odot a_2,\qquad
 y^{(3)}=b_2\odot a_3,\qquad
 y^{(4)}=b_3.
\]
Then \(\Forr=\sigma\Forr^{\rm ung}\) exactly, so the two variables have
the same variance.  Conditional on \(G_1,G_2,G_3\), all coordinates of
the four vectors \(y^{(b)}\) are independent, but generally biased.
For one independent sign \(Y\) with \(\Pr(Y=1)=p\), a multilinear
function satisfies, conditional on all other coordinates,
\[
 \operatorname{Var}_Y(F)
 =4p(1-p)(\partial_YF)^2
 \le(\partial_YF)^2.
\]
Here
\(\partial_YF=(F|_{Y=+1}-F|_{Y=-1})/2\), so this convention agrees with
the coefficient of \(Y\) in the multilinear expansion.
The conditional Efron--Stein inequality therefore gives
\[
 \Var(\Forr^{\rm ung}|G_1,G_2,G_3)
 \le
 \sum_{b,i}
 \E[(\partial_{b,i}\Forr^{\rm ung})^2|G_1,G_2,G_3].
\]
We average this inequality over the latent Gaussians.  For blocks \(1\)
and \(4\), the derivative vector is \(N^{-1/2}\) times a vector of norm
at most one, so the sum of squared derivatives is at most \(1/N\)
pointwise.

For block \(2\), the derivative at coordinate \(j\) has the form
\(\ell_jr_j\), where
\[
 \ell=HD_{a_1}u,
 \qquad
 r=HD_{b_2\odot a_3}HD_{b_3}u.
\]
The vector \(a_1\) is independent of \((b_2,a_3,b_3)\), hence
\(\ell\) and \(r\) are independent.  Marginally, \(a_1\) is an i.i.d.
uniform sign vector, so \(\E\ell_j^2=1/N\), while \(\|r\|\le1\).
Therefore
\[
 \E\sum_j\ell_j^2r_j^2\le\frac1N.
\]
For block \(3\), the left partial-chain vector depends on
\((a_1,b_1,a_2)\), while the right vector \(HD_{b_3}u\) depends only on
the independent i.i.d. uniform sign vector \(b_3\); the same argument
gives another \(1/N\).  These are unconditional calculations after the
conditional Efron--Stein inequality has been averaged; no conditional
uniformity is asserted.  Consequently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:round-var}
 \E\Var(\Forr|G_1,G_2,G_3)
 =\E\Var(\Forr^{\rm ung}|G_1,G_2,G_3)
 \le\frac4N.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Latent Gaussian variance}

Gaussian Poincare gives
\[
 \Var(\overline F)
 \le
 \sum_{s=1}^3
 \E\norm{\nabla_{G_s}\overline F}^2.
\]
For one link, write
\[
 \overline F
 =
 \langle p,D_{\phi(G)}HD_{\phi(HG)}q\rangle,
\]
where \(p,q\) depend on disjoint collections of the other links, are
independent of \(G\) and of each other, and have norm at most one.  Define
\[
 A_i=p_i(HD_{\phi(HG)}q)_i,
 \qquad
 B_j=q_j(HD_{\phi(G)}p)_j.
\]
Because \(H\) is symmetric,
\[
 \nabla_G\overline F
 =
 D_{\phi'(G)}A+HD_{\phi'(HG)}B,
\]
and therefore
\begin{equation}\label{eq:grad}
 \norm{\nabla_G\overline F}^2
 \le
 2L^2(\norm A^2+\norm B^2).
\end{equation}

We use the translation symmetries of the Sylvester matrix.  For
\(s\in\F_2^d\), let
\[
 (P_sv)_i=v_{i+s},
 \qquad
 (D_sv)_i=(-1)^{\langle s,i\rangle}v_i.
\]
Then
\[
 P_sH=HD_s,
 \qquad
 HP_s=D_sH.
\]
Since \(\phi\) is odd,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:M-sym}
       M(P_sg)=P_sM(g),
       \qquad
       M(D_sg)=M(g)P_s.
\end{equation}
The standard Gaussian law is invariant under every \(P_s,D_s\).  It
follows from \eqref{eq:M-sym} that every nonempty left or right
partial-chain vector in \eqref{eq:Fbar} has a distribution invariant
under the transitive translation group \(\{P_s\}\).  Its coordinate
second moments are equal and, because its norm is at most one, are at
most \(1/N\).

At each link, \(p\) is either \(u\) or such a coordinate-transitive
partial-chain vector; the same holds for \(q\).  The vector \(p\) is
independent of \(G,q\), and \(q\) is independent of \(G,p\).  Therefore,
writing \(v=HD_{\phi(HG)}q\),
\[
 \E\norm A^2
 =
 \sum_i\E\!\left[|p_i|^2|v_i|^2\right]
 \le
 \frac1N\E\norm v^2
 \le\frac1N.
\]
The same calculation, with
\(w=HD_{\phi(G)}p\), gives
\[
       \E\norm B^2\le\frac1N.
\]
Equation \eqref{eq:grad} gives
\[
       \E\norm{\nabla_G\overline F}^2
       \le\frac{4L^2}{N}.
\]
Summing the three links,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:latent-var}
       \Var(\overline F)\le\frac{12L^2}{N}.
\end{equation}

By total variance, \eqref{eq:round-var}, \eqref{eq:latent-var}, and
\(L^2<128\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:total-var}
 \Var_{\mu_\sigma}(\Forr)
 \le
 \frac{4+12L^2}{N}
 <
 \frac{1540}{N}.
\end{equation}

\section{Transfer to the exact promises}

Equations \eqref{eq:margin} and \eqref{eq:total-var}, followed by
Chebyshev, give
\[
 \mu_\sigma\left(
 \sigma\Forr<\frac14
 \right)
 \le
 \frac{1540\cdot260^2}{N}
 =
 \frac{104104000}{N}.
\]
For \(N\ge2^{30}\), put
\[
 \varepsilon_0
 =\frac{104104000}{2^{30}}
 =\frac{1626625}{16777216}.
\]
Define
\[
 \nu_\sigma
 =
 \left.\mu_\sigma\right|_{\{\sigma\Forr\ge1/4\}}.
\]
Then
\[
 \nu_+(\Pi_+)=1,
 \qquad
 \nu_-(\Pi_-)=1,
 \qquad
 \TV(\mu_\sigma,\nu_\sigma)\le\varepsilon_0.
\]

Let \(f(x)\) be the probability that the protocol outputs \(+\).
Worst-case success at least \(2/3\) implies
\[
 \E_{\nu_+}f\ge\frac23,
 \qquad
 \E_{\nu_-}f\le\frac13.
\]
Since \(0\le f\le1\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hard-gap}
 \E_{\mu_+}f-\E_{\mu_-}f
 \ge
 \frac13-2\varepsilon_0
 =\frac{3508733}{25165824}.
\end{equation}
On the other hand,
\[
 \frac{3508733}{25165824}
 -\frac{14641}{109375}
 =\frac{15314842691}{2752512000000}>0.
\]
If \(D<\frac{2}{15}N^{1/8}\), Proposition~\ref{prop:ind} therefore
contradicts \eqref{eq:hard-gap}.  Hence
\[
       D\ge\frac{2}{15}N^{1/8}.
\]
This proves Theorem~\ref{thm:main}.

\section{Full-model audit}

The preceding proof applies to the full model in the problem statement.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=2em]
\item
Arbitrary mixed inputs are purified within the same maximum-photon
support.  Arbitrary bypass idlers remain in the ancilla factors.
\item
The slot embedding is exact for maximum sign-mode photon number on
support.  Repeated occupation, bosonic symmetrization, number
superpositions, and vacuum coherence are all retained.
\item
Each batch contains one direct-sum sign-mask interaction.  No known
Hadamard or other processing is inserted between the four masks inside a
batch.
\item
Only a classical outcome controls the next fresh state.  Freshness is
used in two coupled parts of Lemma~\ref{lem:cut}: the marked parallel
slots admit one genuine separating cut, and every continuation acts on a
new tensor factor, giving the factor
\(I_{\cE_{h,y}}\otimes E_{hy}\) in \eqref{eq:pullback}.  Neither the
cut projections nor this factor may be replaced by a claim that the
fully propagated terminal vectors remain orthogonal; Remark~\ref{rem:not-identity}
shows why.
\item
All outcome weights are raw outcome weights.  Heralding and
postselection never divide out reach probability and never discount
dose.
\item
The last-occurrence partition is exact: after omitting each marked toggle,
the same-side residual parity is projected to be disjoint from the exposed
coordinates.  Together with residual-parity equality, this forces the
marked side to be the odd side and prevents duplicate records.
\item
Finite approximation covers arbitrary finite or standard-Borel outcome
spaces, singular histories, and unrestricted width.
\item
Every positive traversal is counted in the branchwise dose.  Because
positive charges are integers, a finite-dose branch has at most \(D\)
positive-charge nodes.  Zero-charge tails are input-independent
conditional on the current classical history and are absorbed into a
terminal kernel, with nontermination treated as a terminal outcome.
\item
Private classical randomness is included as zero-charge classical
branching.
\item
The hard distribution uses the exact normalized Hadamard matrix and the
exact coefficient \(N^{-5/2}\).  The transfer conditions on the exact
thresholds \(+1/4\) and \(-1/4\).
\item
The last step uses worst-case success on every promised input, not an
average-case correctness assumption.
\item
The conclusion is a branchwise hard-dose lower bound.  It is not a
mean-dose bound, does not cover a quantum memory carried between batches,
and does not permit coherent inter-mask processing inside one fresh batch.
\end{itemize}

\section{Standard Gaussian facts used above}

For completeness, we record the three standard Gaussian facts invoked in
the proof.

\subsection{Arcsine identity}

If \(X,Y\) are standard real Gaussians of correlation \(r\), rotational
symmetry in the plane gives
\[
       \E[\operatorname{sgn}X\,\operatorname{sgn}Y]
       =\frac2\pi\arcsin r.
\]

\subsection{Second quantization}

Let \(X\in\mathbb R^a\), \(Y\in\mathbb R^b\) be jointly standard Gaussian
with cross-covariance \(R\).  After singular-value decomposing \(R\) and
making orthogonal changes of variables, the normalized multivariate
Hermite basis diagonalizes the conditional-expectation operator
\(g(Y)\mapsto\E[g(Y)|X]\).  On homogeneous chaos degree \(p\), every
singular value is a product of \(p\) singular values of \(R\), so the
operator norm is at most \(\norm R^p\).

\subsection{Gaussian Poincare}

For a locally Lipschitz function \(F\) of a standard Gaussian vector,
\[
       \Var(F)\le\E\norm{\nabla F}^2.
\]
The function in \eqref{eq:Fbar} is smooth and bounded, so the inequality
applies directly.
